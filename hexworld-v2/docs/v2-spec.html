⬡ HexWorld v2 — Geologic World Generation

Design spec · 2026-08-04 · builds on the HexWorld demo · status: complete — Milestones 1–7 shipped (tectonic worlds, six themes, packaged)

This is the working spec for the next iteration of the HexWorld generator: moving from "which country polygon is this hex in" / "Gaussian blob continents" to a geologically plausible procedural world — real elevation with a true sea level, climate with rain shadows, rivers that flow from sources to mouths (or die honestly in deserts), biomes, themes, and a legend. It exists because of the conversation below.

⚒ Build progress (updated 2026-08-04 — M7 handoff, arc complete)

Live now at hexworld-v2/index.html: the Earth | Procedural mode toggle. Procedural mode has every cell as real data (ocean included), signed elevation with sea level as a true percentile zero (the Ocean % slider delivers the exact land fraction on every seed), a Continents slider (1–7) where the requested number of prominent, separated continents actually materializes (regression-tested), per-continent characters (Alpine / Frozen / Arid / Temperate / Lush / Plains — relief acts today, climate activates in M2), landmasses labeled by flood-fill (no more Voronoi seams), speck-island culling, a hypsometric palette with a live legend, and 28 deterministic tests.

M2 Climate (landed 2026-08-04): every hex now carries temperature (latitude insolation minus a mountain lapse rate) and rainfall from a wind-ordered moisture sweep — parcels evaporate over ocean, rain out over land, and dump on windward slopes with a 60/40 leeward carry, so rain shadows and ~28° desert belts are emergent, not painted. Trade winds / westerlies / polar easterlies flip by latitude belt. Land is labeled by a Whittaker-style biome table (11 biomes: ice cap → tundra → taiga → steppe → desert → forests → savanna → jungle, plus alpine above the treeline) with a nearest-point fallback and maritime coastal moderation; the legend and inspector show biome, °C and rain per hex. The continent characters finally act on climate: a Frozen continent renders as an ice cap with a tundra fringe, Arid reads tan, Lush deep green. Two fun bugs the tests caught: subsidence that only scales rain defers it instead of removing it (the desert belt never formed until descending air actually drank the column dry), and fBm surface roughness was orographically bleeding parcels dry within ~5 cells (every interior turned desert) until sub-range bumps stopped counting as uplift. 49 deterministic tests.

M2.5 gate feedback (2026-08-04): a Warp slider (0–150) — 0 gives round noise blobs, 150 craggy fjord-land (the requested continent count is guaranteed through 110; beyond that heavy warp can merge continents into a Pangaea, on purpose); a sea-breeze lushness pass (rainfall ×1.35 on shoreline hexes, ×1.17 one ring in — green coasts, deserts pushed inland; land mix now ~20% desert / 38% grassland / 34% forest); and a responsive mobile UI — on phones the map is full-bleed with a bottom bar and bottom-sheet controls/legend, on foldables the cards collapse to pills, touch targets are 44px+, breakpoints down to 340px-wide screens.

M3 Hydrology (landed 2026-08-04): rivers are real now — and they flow along hex edges, not through cell centers. Every corner of the hex grid is a junction; a priority-flood pass (the standard terrain-hydrology trick) finds the water surface, each junction gets one seeded downhill outflow, and the M2 rainfall field feeds flow accumulation down those chains. The top slice of flux becomes rivers with width ∝ √flux, so tributaries visibly join into trunks, and because junctions ARE coastline vertices, every river ends at a genuine mouth on the coast — or dies honestly inland: closed basins run an evaporation check (inflow vs lake area), and a starved basin becomes an endorheic salt lake or a dry salt flat — the "river dies in the desert" ask, verbatim. Open basins overflow at the rim and the river continues. Cold high peaks now carry snowcaps above the lapse-rate snowline. The inspector shows per-hex flux; the legend gains Lake and Salt Flat; stats count rivers reaching a mouth. All of it is deterministic per seed (the only randomness is a positional hash per junction for how rivers wander) — 66 tests.

M4 Refine + reject (landed 2026-08-04): the maps clean up after themselves now. Biome boundaries are dithered — a positional jitter on each hex's (temperature, rainfall) before classification — so the ruler-straight banding lines dissolve into organic transitions. Then a Potts/MRF relaxation (a few ICM sweeps over a designer-editable biome-affinity matrix) absorbs single-hex speckle and buffers climate whiplash like desert kissing jungle, while a climate-fit anchor term makes sure rain shadows survive the polish (regression-tested). And the "many seeds unusable" complaint dies by construction: every generated world must pass a quality floor (enough rivers reaching a mouth, enough distinct biomes, the requested continents actually present) — a failing seed silently retries seed+1 and the stats bar says exactly why ("seed N rejected: only 2 river mouths…"). From the M3 feedback round: a Min-lake slider (1–8) — basins that flood fewer hexes than the dial never pool, so the 1-hex speck ponds are gone at the default of 3 (slide to 1 to get them back); starved specks still kill their river, they just don't leave a pan. Warp keeps its full 0–150 range — the Pangaea zone past 110 stays, and the seed validator knows to stand down there. 83 tests.

M5 Themes + Advanced drawer (landed 2026-08-04): the world generator grew six personalities. A Theme picker on the main bar — Terran (the Earthlike default, byte-identical to what you saw yesterday), Pangaea (one supercontinent with a dry endorheic heart), Shattered Archipelago (a dozen-plus island masses), Dune (a desert planet where the rare lakes render as oases and rivers are allowed to be scarce), Glacial (an ice age: lowered sea level opens land bridges, meltwater lakes, huge caps) and Primordial (young, jagged, wet — its peaks re-skin as Volcanic Peaks until real plate margins arrive in M6). Each theme is a bundle: dial defaults + a whole-world climate character + a palette re-skin + its own acceptance rules — a Dune world is allowed to be river-poor, an Archipelago world MUST actually shatter, and every theme hashes the seed into its own seed universe (same seed number, six different planets — same hex lattice though, which is a nice determinism party trick). Below the main bar, the Advanced drawer: Warp, Min lake, Mountains (a vertical-exaggeration curve on land elevation — raw amplitude turned out to be eaten by normalization, a fun probe), Rainfall ×, River density, River honesty (width ∝ √flux is the honest truth; slide down to flatter minor rivers — selection was already honest by construction, a monotone transform can't change a percentile cut), and a sea-level fine offset. Picking a theme re-seats every dial to its envelope; any dial you touch afterwards overrides the theme. Gate call: Continents stays on the main bar next to Ocean — they're the two levers that define a world. 98 tests.

M6 Tectonics (landed 2026-08-04): the placeholder continent masks are gone — worlds are now made by supercontinent breakup. One landmass grows in pre-drift "plate space", cracks along a shared meandering rift network, and the fragments drift apart on the wrapped map; because both sides of every rift are cut by the same crack curve, facing coastlines are conjugate — they visibly fit, the "once-connected" look from the original brief. Fragments carry real plate anatomy: leading edges are active margins (a coastal mountain arc with a deep-ocean trench offshore, Andes-style) and rifted/trailing edges are passive margins (wide shallow shelf seas, Atlantic-style) — look for the pale shelf halos vs the dark trench bands. Active margins unlock the first genuinely new biome since the climate engine: Volcanic — dark basalt-red cells where subduction coasts rise high, in the legend whenever a world has them. Primordial now uses the real thing (its alpine re-skin is retired; it ships as a just-broken 3-fragment world, leading edges ablaze), and Pangaea has zero volcanoes by construction — no drift, no subduction. Crowded worlds (6–7 continents) get a barely-drifted young rift instead of wide oceans — there is genuinely no room on the cylinder, which is its own kind of world. The Continents slider is now literally the fragment count. Getting the "requested continents actually materialize" guarantee to survive real plate mechanics took seven probed mechanism fixes (drift must be a uniform expansion; rotation needs drift room; wiggle the crack distance, never the Voronoi domain…) — the kind of fight you only win with a regression suite. 112 tests, zero rejected seeds across all six themes × seeds 1–10.

M7 Package & handoff (landed 2026-08-04): the wrap-up milestone. The lab's doc set (README, agent instructions, design spec) was rewritten to describe the real M1–M6 engine — the pipeline map, every dial, the determinism contract, the headless QA hooks, and the hard-won gotchas — framed as a standalone folder that runs from any static host or straight off disk. Size budget confirmed: the whole world generator ships in a single 1.02MB index.html (budget 1.3MB), zero runtime requests. Final QA passed headlessly in both modes.

Post-release: the Aridity dial (2026-08-05): a new Advanced-drawer slider for "less desert, more Hawaiian islands". It scales the two mechanisms that MAKE deserts — the horse-latitude subsidence belt (both the rain suppression and the column drain) and the Arid continent-character intensity — so 0% gives lush island-jungle worlds (terran desert share drops ~16% → ~3% of land) and 200% deepens hyper-desert. Rain-shadow deserts stay at every setting: the dry leeward Kona side of a volcanic island is earned by the wind model, not painted. Pairs naturally with Shattered Archipelago + a nudge of Rainfall. 119 tests.

That's a wrap — v2 shipped, Milestones 1–7 complete. Everything beyond (spherical projection, erosion, seasons…) stays deliberately parked until there's a new spec.

— Ensure geographical entities retain real, practical form. Example: Rivers should be sourced from things like snow and rainfall, subterranean sources, or streams and should primarily have a mouth. 2 · What we diagnosed in the current procedural generator

We read the deployed generator's source. It's a solid foundation (seeded simplex noise, domain warping, seamless east–west wrap), and every symptom Spyder001 noticed traces to a specific, fixable root cause:

SymptomRoot causeFix in v2
"Sea level doesn't respect boundaries"; slider feels random Sea level is an absolute threshold on an unnormalized field, fighting random continent-attractor strengths (0.35–0.80 vs a 0.35 default threshold) Percentile sea level: sort elevations, cut at the Ocean % dial → exact land fraction on every seed, and elevation becomes signed with a true zero
"Weird continents" / straight borders through one landmass Continent identity = Voronoi cell of the nearest attractor, computed independently of the actual landmass (the "SQL join" comment in the code is this additive-attractor blend) Label landmasses by flood fill of connected land; in v2 continents are drifted fragments of one supercontinent, so identity is real history
Diffuse, blobby land masses Continents are Gaussian blobs; the fractal noise term (×0.35) is too weak to disguise them, and overlapping blobs add without saturating Noise becomes the dominant term; tectonic fragments + variance-gated ridge belts shape the outline; domain warp applied to the whole field
No rivers / biomes / terrain possible Ocean doesn't exist as data — only land hexes are stored, so moisture, coasts, lakes, and river mouths have nothing to flow through Every cell exists (ocean included) and carries elevation, temperature, rainfall, water state, biome
"Many seeds are unusable" Nothing checks the output; land fraction is an emergent accident Hard validation (Dwarf Fortress style): every world is checked against the theme's acceptance rules; failures auto-retry with the next internal seed and tell you why ("seed 42 rejected: no river reaches a mouth")

3 · Research digest

PerfectWorld (the Civ mod, read from its actual source)

  • Percentile thresholds everywhere. Sea level, hills, mountains, desert, plains, forest, rivers — every cut is a percentile of the sorted map. Composition ("15% of land is mountain") becomes a design decision, identical on every seed. The single highest-value idea.
  • Rain shadow from one subtraction. Rainfall is a single sweep in wind order: air loads moisture over ocean, and rain falls in proportion to the increase in uplift along the path. Windward slope → torrential; leeward → the term clamps to ~0 and the dry airmass marches inland. Nobody codes "rain shadow" as a rule — it emerges.
  • Latitude bands do climate for free. Uplift peaks at the equator and the polar front (~60°), bottoms at ~28° — which is why Earth's great deserts sit at 30°. One piecewise curve makes maps read Earth-like instantly.
  • Rivers on hex edges as a drainage tree. Fill depressions → each junction gets exactly one downhill outflow (chosen with seeded randomness so rivers meander instead of running straight) → accumulate flow → keep the top percentile. Real dendritic networks.
  • Relief by local difference, not absolute altitude — otherwise you get peak-blobs surrounded by donuts of hills instead of linear ranges with foothills.

Shared principles from Minecraft, No Man's Sky, Dwarf Fortress, Civ, Red Blob

  • Fields first, labels second (Minecraft 1.18's entire redesign): compute continuous elevation/temperature/moisture, then classify biomes from them — never the reverse.
  • Parameter-space biome table with nearest-match fallback: biomes are boxes in (elevation × temperature × rainfall …) space, as plain editable data; the lookup can never fail; each theme swaps in its own table.
  • Archetype-first sampling (No Man's Sky planet classes): pick the theme, then jitter within its parameter envelope — variety is bounded by construction, not luck. This is exactly Art's "each theme has its own subset of seeds": hash(seed, theme).
  • Hard rejection (Dwarf Fortress): "world rejected: too few rivers" — regenerate and say why. Belt and braces on top of soft shaping.
  • One seed, salted sub-seeds: every subsystem draws from hash(masterSeed, "climate", …) so subsystems are independent and debuggable (same continents, different rivers, as an authoring feature).
  • Endorheic basins: ~20% of Earth's land drains to no ocean. If a basin's inflow ≤ its evaporation, the river ends in a terminal salt lake — Art's "river dies in the desert", straight from real hydrology.
  • Scale is a non-issue: Red Blob's mapgen4 runs 1M+ cells in browser JS. Our finest grid is ~63k hexes; the whole pipeline budgets 300–600 ms.

The key mathematical finding: logarithmic falloff is free

Art asked for neighbor influence that drops off logarithmically with distance. It turns out a classical result covers this: a multi-octave fractal noise field with amplitude ∝ 1/frequency (persistence 0.5, lacunarity 2 — the standard settings) is the de Wijs process / 2D Gaussian Free Field, whose spatial correlation decays exactly logarithmically. And on the hex graph, summing equal-weight smoothing passes at radii 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 approximates a log kernel at O(N·log R) cost. The requirement is satisfied by construction, twice, for milliseconds — no exotic model needed.

4 · The design decisions (as asked and answered)

Q1 — Which probability model drives terrain assignment? (the requested top-3)
Hybrid: noise backbone + MRF polish — recommended, chosen
Pure noise + parameter-space lookup (Minecraft 1.18 style) alone
MCRF / transiogram spatial Markov chain

Wave Function Collapse was evaluated and rejected for the whole-map role (no metric distance → nowhere to put a falloff kernel; unbounded restart cost at this scale); it stays in reserve as an optional local detailer.

Why the hybrid won Log falloff free (see §3) · "what likes to touch what" is directly tunable — a K×K affinity matrix in JSON where "mountain likes mountain" is one number · fixes box-boundary artifacts (jungle abutting tundra) and speckle · ~300–600 ms worst case, no unbounded retries · every stage independently testable
Costs accepted Most stages to build (~6 modules, front-loaded work) · soft constraints only ("never adjacent" is very unlikely, not impossible) · two tuning surfaces (biome table + affinity matrix) can fight if the pairwise term is set too strong — guarded by a regression test that a known rain shadow survives the polish pass
Q2 — How do continents form, so landmasses look once-connected?
Supercontinent breakup (Wilson cycle) — recommended, chosen: grow one Pangaea, crack it along a rift network, drift the fragments apart. Conjugate coastlines fit like puzzle pieces by construction. Rifted (passive) coasts get wide shelves and deltas; colliding (active) coasts get mountain ranges.
Grown plates (BFS flood-fill tectonics) — geologically-placed mountains, but continents don't literally fit together
Noise-ridge faking (PerfectWorld's approach) — cheapest, but no connectedness story
Q3 — Keep the real-Earth country map?
Keep both — chosen: an Earth | Procedural mode toggle. Earth mode stays byte-identical (regression-locked); Procedural is the new generator.
Procedural only
Q4 — How do the new parameters surface in the UI?
Theme + core dials + collapsible Advanced drawer — chosen. Main bar: Theme, Seed, Ocean %, plus the existing density and node-cluster buttons. Advanced drawer: mountain %, rainfall multiplier, river density, fragment count, sea-level fine offset, honest-rivers dial. Legend always visible.
Curated minimal (theme + seed + ocean % only)
Full dial board (every parameter exposed)
Q5 (added at the Milestone-1 review) — Sporadic land vs defined continents?
Continents slider + per-continent character — Art's call after seeing the first renders: a 1–7 continent count (each count is its own sub-seed universe), with each continent drawing a personality (snowy / desert / mountainous / …).

Implementation note: the naive version of this (Gaussian attractor blobs) is exactly what produced "weird continents" before. What shipped instead: best-candidate site placement, saturating warped-distance masks, and a ratio-based rift — each cell is suppressed by (second-nearest ÷ nearest) continent influence, which ranks the zones between continents below outward-facing coasts. Because sea level is a percentile, it's the height ordering that decides where land grows — so continents grow outward instead of bridging into blobs. A regression test asserts the requested count materializes across seeds.

5 · The design

Every hex is multi-layered data

Unlike v1 (land hexes only), every cell exists — ocean included — and carries:

{ elevation   signed, 0 = sea level (the true zero)
  crust       continental | oceanic
  fragmentId  which piece of the broken supercontinent
  temperature latitude insolation − altitude lapse (snow-capped equatorial peaks work)
  rainfall    from the wind-advection sweep
  water       none | ocean | coast | lake | saltflat
  riverEdges  which of the 6 edges carry a river (width ∝ √flow)
  biome       from the theme's parameter-space table
  features    snowcap · forest · delta · … }

Land hosts lakes and rivers above it; mountains host snow above the snowline — Art's layering requirement, expressed as data.

The generation pipeline

0 SEED       master seed + theme → salted sub-seeds per stage; seed jitters within
             the theme's envelope (each theme = its own universe of seeds)
1 TECTONICS  one supercontinent → rift cracks → fragments drift apart
             → coastlines that FIT, passive vs active margins recorded
2 FIELDS     elevation = crust base + collision uplift + fractal detail + ridge belts
             → sea level cut at the Ocean % percentile → SIGNED elevation
3 CLIMATE    temperature from latitude − altitude lapse
             rainfall: one sweep along the prevailing winds — evaporate over ocean,
             rain on rising ground, pass the rest downwind → RAIN SHADOWS emerge,
             deserts appear at ~28° latitude on their own
4 HYDROLOGY  fill depressions → every river junction gets one downhill outflow
             → accumulate flow → rivers on hex edges, width ∝ √flow
             → basins that evaporate more than they receive stay closed:
               terminal salt lakes, rivers that die in the desert
5 LABEL      biome = lookup in the theme's (elevation × temp × rainfall) table,
             nearest-match fallback, dithered boundaries (no hard contour lines)
6 REFINE     3–5 relaxation sweeps with the K×K adjacency-affinity matrix
             (the neighbor-probability model, with log-distance influence)
7 FEATURES   snowcaps, forests, river-mouth deltas; cosmetic fixups last
8 VALIDATE   check the theme's acceptance rules; on failure retry internally and
             SHOW the reason — no more unusable seeds reaching the screen

UI & legend

Everything the v1 demo does (pan, wheel/pinch zoom, hover, click-to-select, node clustering 1/7/19, density presets) is preserved. New: the Earth|Procedural toggle, the Theme select, Ocean % dial, the Advanced drawer — and a proper legend: a color swatch + name for every biome and feature on the current map, driven by the active theme's palette (colors finally mean something instead of being hashed from names). The inspector gains elevation, biome, rainfall, temperature, and river flow.

6 · Build milestones

Each milestone ends with tests green, a rebuilt single-file index.html, and rendered PNGs for visual review before advancing. All seven landed 2026-08-04.

  1. Signed world — every cell exists, fractal elevation, percentile sea level + Ocean % dial, mode toggle, hypsometric palette + first legend
  2. Climate — temperature + wind-advected moisture; rain shadows visible; first biomes
  3. Hydrology — rivers with real mouths, closed salt-lake basins, snowcaps
  4. Refine + reject — adjacency polish + seed validation with surfaced reasons
  5. Themes — all six, each with its own table, palette, and acceptance rules
  6. Tectonics — supercontinent breakup replaces the placeholder landmass (the riskiest stage lands last, on a proven pipeline)

7 · Verification

  • Deterministic: same (seed, theme, dials) ⇒ identical world, tested by deep equality.
  • Earth mode byte-identical to today's demo (regression-locked baselines).
  • Every river terminates at an ocean or a terminal lake — asserted, not hoped.
  • A known rain shadow must survive the refinement pass (physics beats polish).
  • The noise field's measured influence-vs-distance curve is checked against ln(h).
  • Headless browser checks: both modes load with zero console errors; screenshot matrix across themes × densities.
